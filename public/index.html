🎬 Real Workplace Scenarios
Click to see examples and what makes them harassment

▼ Scenario 1: Physical Harassment

Physical sexual harassment includes unwelcome physical behaviour such as touching, deliberately brushing against someone, hugging, kissing, patting, pinching, stroking, or other unwanted physical advances.

Situation: A manager repeatedly pats a colleague on the shoulder and stands too close, even after the colleague visibly moves away.

Why it's harassment: Unwelcome physical contact continues despite non-verbal signals to stop.

▼ Scenario 2: Verbal Harassment

Situation: A colleague makes inappropriate jokes in team meetings and also comments on a female employee’s appearance, “You look very attractive today. I am sure people would just be looking at you instead of working”

Why it's harassment: The repeated inappropriate jokes and comments about her appearance are unwelcome, make her feel uncomfortable and embarrassed,

▼ Scenario 3: Digital Harassment

Situation: A colleague sends inappropriate messages via WhatsApp and shares explicit memes in the office group.

Why it's harassment: Unsolicited inappropriate content (sexual or non sexual) in workplace communication channels.

▼ Scenario 4: Quid Pro Quo

Situation: A supervisor says, "Go out with me tonight and I will approve your leave request tomorrow."

Why it's harassment: Employment benefits are linked to sexual compliance — the most serious form.

▼ Scenario 5: Hostile Environment

Situation: A colleague repeatedly makes remarks suggesting that a female employee received her promotion because of her looks. He also targets her at work by constantly belittling her performance and making her feel that she is less capable and valued than her colleagues.

Why it's harassment: Repeated degrading and discriminatory remarks can create a hostile, intimidating, and uncomfortable work place atmosphere.

▼ Scenario 6: Non-Verbal Harassment

Situation: A colleague makes unwelcome eye contact, leers, or makes suggestive facial expressions or hand gestures towards a team member during meetings. In another situation, a colleague may simply stare at a team member without any sexual intention and may not realise that he is staring or that his behaviour is making the other person feel uncomfortable.

Why it's harassment: Non-verbal conduct, whether or not it conveys sexual intent, can create an uncomfortable or hostile work environment and make the aggrieved employee feel uncomfortable, intimidated and unsafe.

Sexual harassment does not always involve a direct or explicit request for sex. Behaviour can be sexual or non-sexual in nature, but what matters is how the behaviour is experienced, the context in which it happens, and whether it is unwelcome.

⚠️ Good Intent Doesn't Prevent Harassment
Understanding Impact vs Intent

📌 The Situation:

During meetings, a Manager often makes comments about a female employee that are linked to her gender. For example, “You’re too emotional to handle this project.” Instead of addressing her ideas or the way the discussion is being handled, he dismisses her based on a stereotype that women are overly emotional. Similarly, when she disagrees with him, he jokes, “Must be that time of the month.” Manager believes that he is just joking and doesn't mean any harm. But for the employee, these comments are uncomfortable and hurtful. She starts feeling singled out and stereotyped because she is a woman.

Over time, she becomes hesitant to speak up or share her opinions in meetings because she doesn't want to hear another comment like this.

🔍 Analysis:

❌ MANAGER'S INTENT

Manager claims humor, not harm. Says "Don't be so sensitive" or "Everyone knows I'm joking." Believes it's harmless banter and employee is overreacting.

✓ IMPACT ON EMPLOYEE

Employee feels disrespected, stereotyped, and unsafe. Stops contributing ideas to avoid jokes. Loses confidence. Workplace becomes hostile based on gender stereotypes. Creates chilling effect on participation.

🎯 VERDICT: This IS HARASSMENT

Type: Verbal harassment, gender-based

It is the IMPACT or EFFECT of the behavior on the Aggrieved EMPLOYEE and NOT the INTENT of the Respondent that is CRITICAL in an assessment of harassment cases.

💡 KEY PRINCIPLE: Good intentions do not excuse harassment. What matters is the impact on the employee, not the intent of the perpetrator.

👤 Employee Responsibilities
What You Should Do
  • Be aware of inappropriate behaviours and avoid them
  • Know the Policy on sexual harassment thoroughly
  • Say "NO" to situations that make you uncomfortable
  • Trust your instincts and walk away when needed
  • Record details — dates, times, places, and witnesses if harassment continues
  • Report inappropriate behaviour whether to you or colleagues
  • Keep confidentiality and share only with right authorities

🎯 Your voice matters! Speaking up protects you, your colleagues, and the workplace.

👔 Manager Responsibilities
Leadership in Creating a Safe Workplace
  • Educate everyone — Ensure all employees know the Policy and professional behaviour standards
  • Model respect — Be a role model for sensitive, inclusive behaviour
  • Act on every incident — Never stay silent if you observe or hear about harassment
  • Listen with empathy — Never trivialise a complaint; make no judgments
  • Manage confidentiality — Handle complaints proactively and sensitively
  • Prevent retaliation - Ensure no retaliation against aggrieved employee or witnesses
  • Document appropriately — Keep records of any incidents or complaints
  • Follow procedures — Escalate to IC/HR as required by policy

💡 Pro Tip: When someone comes forward, thank them for their courage. This encourages others to report and creates psychological safety.

📝 How to Report a Complaint
Step-by-Step Reporting Process
1️⃣
Prepare Your Complaint

Document date, time, location, witnesses (if any), and detailed nature of the incident(s).

2️⃣
Submit to IC

Email your written complaint to: complaintscommittee@azafashions.com

3️⃣
Meet the Deadline

File within 3 months of the incident (or last incident in a series). IC may extend by another 3 months for valid reasons.

4️⃣
Get Acknowledgement

IC will acknowledge receipt and inform you of the next steps. Your complaint will be treated with utmost confidentiality.

5️⃣
Cooperate with Inquiry

Participate in the investigation process. You will be kept informed of progress (while maintaining confidentiality of other parties).

⚠️ Important: DO NOT publicize alleged harassment. Report only to the IC. Breach of confidentiality will be treated as separate misconduct.

⚖️ The Investigation Process
Key Timelines and Procedures
7️⃣
IC Notifies Respondent

Within 7 days: IC forwards a copy of the complaint to the person accused of harassment (the Respondent).

1️⃣0️⃣
Respondent Submits Response

Within 10 days: Respondent must file their written response with evidence and list of witnesses.

9️⃣0️⃣
IC Completes Inquiry

Within 90 days: IC conducts a fair, thorough inquiry and submits findings and recommendations.

1️⃣0️⃣
Findings Sent to Management

Within 10 days: IC sends findings and recommendations to Management for action.

6️⃣0️⃣
Management Action

Within 60 days: Management must act on IC's recommendations. Possible outcomes: warnings, demotion, withholding promotion, or termination.

📌 Note: Neither the Aggrieved Employee nor the Respondent can be represented by a lawyer during IC proceedings.

🛡️ Your Protections
Rights Guaranteed Under the Law

🚫 Protection Against Retaliation

Every employee who reports harassment or participates in an investigation has the right to be protected. An aggrieved employee should not face pressure, punishment, exclusion, unfair treatment, or any negative consequences for speaking up or seeking help. Retaliation can include isolation or exclusion, demotion, threats, unfair treatment, or negative performance appraisals because an employee has made a complaint or supported an investigation.

🔐 Confidentiality

All employees and IC members must keep every complaint confidential. Incidents are handled sensitively, with utmost privacy for all parties, as far as practically possible. Only need-to-know personnel are informed.

⚖️ Fair Inquiry Process

You have the right to a fair, impartial inquiry conducted by the IC. You can present evidence, call witnesses, and respond to allegations against you. The process is transparent and objective.

📢 Right to Appeal

If you disagree with the IC's findings or if they aren't implemented properly, you have the right to appeal within 90 days under the Act.

⭐ Remember: Mala fide (bad faith) complaints are handled seriously, but lack of evidence alone does NOT mean a complaint was made in bad faith.
